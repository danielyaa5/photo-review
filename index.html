What changes in the copies

A Live Photo whose still is HEIC keeps that still exactly as it is; a JPEG still becomes HEIC like any JPEG photo.

JPEG photos become HEIC copies at the chosen resolution and quality, usually a half to three quarters of the size, keeping the HDR of iPhone photos. Re-compressing a JPEG loses a little quality, so keep High if in doubt.

RAW copies are 10-bit HEIC at the chosen resolution and quality, developed with macOS's standard RAW settings. They aren't HDR, and editing them later is like editing any photo, not a RAW file. Their camera details, date and location carry over. A RAW photo with edits in Photos is skipped, because a copy made from the RAW file would lose them, and so is a RAW+JPEG pair.

HDR videos stay HDR, and HDR, ProRes and other 10-bit videos are always saved as HEVC so they keep their full colour depth.

Edits made in Photos are baked into the copies.

Titles, captions and keywords are copied to each new item; people tags can't be. For that, macOS asks once whether Cloud Media Shrinker may control Photos. Without it, originals picked for deletion are kept.

Leave room on your Mac

Downloaded originals can grow the Photos library. Use Optimize Mac Storage in Photos' iCloud settings and smaller batches if space is tight. After checking the smaller copies and their iCloud upload, you can permanently remove only their replaced originals from Recently Deleted. This deletes those originals across your iCloud Photos devices and ends recovery. Photos storage guide
